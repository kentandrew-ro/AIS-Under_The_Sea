The polar seas and the Global Goals

Why the poles matter

The Arctic and the Southern Ocean around Antarctica help cool the planet and feed huge food webs. Warming and fishing pressure make good rules and good science essential.

01 0–10 m

Sea Ice Zone

Where the ocean freezes, and where polar life begins.

Target 14.2 · Protect ecosystems

A floating hunting ground

Sea ice forms when the ocean surface freezes, at about −1.8 °C. In the Arctic, polar bears hunt seals from it. Bright ice reflects sunlight back to space, while dark open water absorbs it, so losing ice speeds up warming.

Does melting sea ice raise sea level?

Hardly. Sea ice is already floating, like ice in a glass. Ice on land, in ice sheets and glaciers, is what adds water to the ocean when it melts.

Ice Status

−13% per decade · Arctic September ice

Shrinking summer ice

NOAA’s Arctic Report Card found that September sea ice, at its yearly minimum, has been shrinking by roughly 13% per decade compared with the 1981–2010 average. What remains is younger and thinner.

02 10–100 m

Under the Ice

A green-tinged ceiling that feeds a swarm.

Target 14.4 · End overfishing

The krill engine

Algae grow on the underside of sea ice, and Antarctic krill graze on it in huge swarms. Krill feed whales, seals, fish and penguins, so krill fishing has to be managed carefully. That job belongs to CCAMLR, the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources.

Why is icefish blood clear?

Antarctic icefish have almost colorless blood with no hemoglobin. Very cold water holds a lot of oxygen, so these fish can get by without it.

03 100–800 m

Shelf Waters

Cold, rich water over the continental shelf.

Target 14.5 · Conserve marine areas

Divers of the cold

Emperor penguins are the only penguins that breed during the Antarctic winter, and they can dive deeper than 500 m for fish and krill. Weddell seals also dive hundreds of metres beneath the ice, while leopard seals lie in wait at the ice edge.

In 2016 members of CCAMLR agreed to create a marine protected area in the Ross Sea region.

04 800 m and deeper

Polar Deep

Dark, cold and quiet, with a slow rain of marine snow.

Target 14.a · Advance ocean science

Arctic divers

Narwhals live in the Arctic and can dive deeper than 1,000 m, hunting in the dark. The long spiral “horn” is really a tooth. Little is known about life in the polar deep, which is why shared ocean science matters.

1959 Antarctic Treaty signed

Target 14.c · International law

Rules for a frozen continent

The Antarctic Treaty reserves Antarctica for peace and science. Later agreements, including CCAMLR, help protect the Southern Ocean around it.

Sustainable Development Goal 14

Protect the
Poles

The poles feel like another world, but what we burn, buy and throw away reaches them. These steps help from anywhere.

Target 14.3

Cut your emissions

Warming is the biggest threat to sea ice. Using less fossil fuel is the most direct way to help: save energy and walk, cycle or share a ride when practical.

Target 14.4

Ask where products come from

Krill is used in some supplements and fish feed. Ask where products come from and look for trusted certifications and well-managed fisheries.

Target 14.5

Support protected areas

Marine protected areas with clear rules and enforcement give polar wildlife room to recover. Back groups and policies that create and enforce them.

Target 14.1

Cut single-use plastic

Plastic travels. Microplastics have been found in polar sea ice, so using fewer disposables helps even far from any coast.
