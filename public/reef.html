Coral reefs and the Global Goals

Why reefs matter

Reefs feed coastal communities, support fisheries and tourism, and protect shorelines from waves. Three SDG 14 targets matter most for them.

01 0–3 m

Lagoon Zone

Calm, shallow water where young fish grow up.

Target 14.2 · Protect ecosystems

A nursery by the shore

Mangrove roots and seagrass meadows shelter young fish, crabs and shrimp before they move out to the reef. Mangroves also trap sediment and soften waves, so protecting them helps the reef next door.

What is a coral, really?

Reef-building corals are tiny animals called polyps. Each one builds a hard skeleton, and thousands together grow into a reef. Much of their color and food comes from microscopic algae living inside them.

Reef Status

≈25% of marine species

A small place with a big crowd

About one quarter of all marine species live in, on or around coral reefs, rivaling the variety found in tropical rainforests.

02 3–10 m

Reef Crest

Waves break here, and the reef takes the hit.

Target 14.2 · Protect ecosystems

The reef as a seawall

The crest faces the waves head on. Healthy reefs can absorb up to 97% of a wave’s energy, which protects shorelines from storms and erosion.

Parrotfish scrape algae off the coral so it is not smothered, and their waste turns into sand.

03 10–30 m

Reef Slope

The busiest neighborhood, full of coral gardens and fish.

Target 14.3 · Ocean acidification

Gardens under pressure

Most coral and fish crowd the sunlit slope, and green sea turtles graze on seagrass and algae nearby. But the slope also feels the effects of heat and acidification. When seawater absorbs carbon dioxide its pH falls, which makes it harder for corals to build their skeletons.

Can a reef recover?

Yes, if the stress eases. Cooler water and fewer local pressures, such as polluted runoff and anchor damage, give corals a better chance to recover.

Reef Status

84% of reef area · 2023–2025

The fourth global bleaching event

In April 2025 NOAA reported that heat stress high enough to cause bleaching had hit about 84% of the world’s reef area since January 2023, the largest bleaching event on record.

04 30–150 m

Mesophotic Reef

A dim reef where only a little sunlight arrives.

Target 14.5 · Conserve marine areas

A deeper refuge?

“Mesophotic” means middle light. Plate corals spread wide to catch the little sunlight that reaches them, and big fish patrol the edges. Deeper water can be cooler, so these reefs may shelter species when the shallows overheat, but they still face fishing, pollution and warming.

How do protected areas help?

Clear rules, monitoring and enforcement let reef habitats and wildlife recover. Target 14.5 aims to conserve at least 10% of coastal and marine areas.

Sustainable Development Goal 14

Protect the
Reef

Reefs are shaped by what happens on land and in the air above them. These habits help them stay healthy.

Target 14.2

Choose reef-friendly sunscreen

Some sunscreen ingredients have been linked to coral stress, and some places restrict them. Check local rules and cover up with a rash guard when you can.

Target 14.2

Give reefs room to grow

When snorkeling or diving, keep your fins and hands clear of coral. Use a designated mooring where available so anchors and chains stay off the reef.

Target 14.4

Choose responsible seafood

Ask what species you are buying and where and how it was caught. Use a seafood guide or trusted certification to check.

Target 14.3

Cut your emissions

Burning fewer fossil fuels means less carbon dioxide warming and acidifying the ocean. Save energy and walk, cycle or share a ride when practical.
